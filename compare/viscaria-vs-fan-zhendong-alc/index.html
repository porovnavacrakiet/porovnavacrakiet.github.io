BLADE COMPARISON · UPDATED AUGUST 2026

Viscaria vs
Fan Zhendong ALC

Two premium outer-ALC blades with a similar offensive direction. The useful decision is about handle, balance and the response you trust under pressure.

BUTTERFLY · OUTER ARYLATE-CARBON · OFFENSIVE SHAKEHAND

SHORT ANSWER

Expect a close family resemblance, not a category change.

  • Both are fast, stable outer-ALC blades for modern topspin play.
  • Viscaria is the long-running reference; Fan Zhendong ALC is the more recent pro-model alternative.
  • Choose the one whose handle and balance let you relax your hand and accelerate freely.

If you already play confidently with one of these blades, switching to the other is unlikely to transform your game overnight. Their shared outer composite concept gives both a crisp, direct response and enough stability for active blocks and counter-topspins. The individual blade weight, handle comfort and your rubbers will normally create the bigger difference.

At the table: Fan Zhendong ALC has a small edge for direct counters and punch blocks; it is not a different speed class.

On receive: Viscaria’s slight control edge may feel calmer, but the handle and actual blade weight can outweigh this narrow gap.

What they have in common

Both models sit in Butterfly’s fast offensive outer-ALC family. With the composite layers close to the surface, the ball leaves the racket relatively early on an active stroke. That design gives a clear, firm contact and a large enough sweet spot for countering pace close to and a step away from the table.

Neither is automatically easier. A player moving from an all-wood or inner-fibre blade may find either one more direct on service return and blocks. A player who already enjoys an outer-ALC response will recognise the same broad character in both.

Where you may feel a difference

Handle shape and how a particular specimen balances in the hand matter more than a catalogue headline. The right grip lets you stay loose through a forehand topspin, change to the backhand quickly and use the wrist on receive. If one handle causes you to squeeze, it is the wrong choice even if its published specification looks ideal.

Actual weight also changes the result. A few grams toward the head can make a blade feel more powerful and stable in a counter, but slower to recover in quick backhand exchanges. Ask a retailer for the measured weight if that detail matters to you.

BLADE VERDICT

Choose by fit, not by the name on the handle.

  • Choose Viscaria if its familiar handle and balance already feel natural, or you want the classic outer-ALC reference.
  • Choose Fan Zhendong ALC if its handle profile gives you a more relaxed grip and better backhand freedom.
  • Keep your current blade if you want a dramatic change: rubber hardness, thickness or blade category will create a clearer difference.

Rubbers decide the final setup

On either blade, a hard hybrid forehand rubber will produce a more deliberate, powerful contact; an elastic European or Japanese topsheet gives easier activation and a livelier first topspin. Using the same rubbers on both blades is the fairest way to discover whether the handle and balance are genuinely better for you.
